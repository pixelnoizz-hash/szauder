This is not just religion, nor just ritual. It is the performance of authority in a society that
no longer believes in singular truths. The bishop's silence is louder than prophecy, his shadow
stitched into the chaos beneath his feet. Each square beneath him is a decision, a doctrine, a
decree — but none leads to resolution.

Politics and religion blend here, not to offer clarity, but to confuse. We build, like Babel's
architects, higher and higher, driven not by vision but inertia. We do. We act. We legislate.
We proclaim. And yet we ask: where does this all lead?

The video doesn't answer. It mirrors. It resists narrative. A frozen frame in a story too complex
to tell — an opera without libretto, a ritual stripped of salvation.
